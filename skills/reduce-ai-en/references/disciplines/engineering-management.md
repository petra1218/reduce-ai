# Discipline: Engineering / Construction Management

The specific hazard in this field: the literature already carries a thick layer of boilerplate, and AI flavor stacks on top of it. Separate the two with one test — delete the phrase; if the sentence loses no information, it was boilerplate.

## Trade vocabulary (keep)

These are the field's working terms; stripping them marks the writer as an outsider:

- Actors and delivery: owner, contractor, supervisor/engineer, EPC, DBB, design-bid-build, construction management at-risk, FIDIC forms, NEC contracts
- Planning and control: WBS, master schedule, critical path (CPM), PERT, float, progress recovery, delay claim, notice of claim
- Earned value: PV/EV/AC, CV/SV, SPI/CPI, EAC, estimate at completion
- Quality and safety: inspection and test plan (ITP), hold points, concealed-work acceptance, safety-critical temporary works
- Tools named as tools: BIM, 4D scheduling, digital twin — used factually, not promoted

## Field boilerplate (cut)

Worst when stacked with generic machine flavor:

- "lean management, cost reduction, and efficiency gains" as a triple — bad writing even when human, mass-produced when machine
- "scientific, standardized, and informatized management" permutations
- "whole-process, all-round, multi-level, multi-dimensional" — dead weight in most positions
- "provides a scientific basis for project management decision-making" — the universal closing unless there is an actual decision and basis to name
- "empower", "synergy", "closed loop", "deep integration" — business-speak, not terminology
- "significant theoretical and practical implications" — the reader decides value from results

## Sources of credibility

Details that make a practitioner believe the author did the work:

- Standards cited specifically: FIDIC 1999 Red Book Clause 20.1, ISO 21500, AIA contract families, ISO 9001 — to the clause, not the cover page
- Project particulars: "a metro line-7 running-tunnel contract, cut-and-cover, twin-box frame, 2×11 m", contract form, contract value magnitude, duration (anonymize to "a major public project in eastern China" if needed — keep the technical parameters)
- Data with a caliber: variance ratios, SPI/CPI values, rework rate, number of sample projects, observation window — numbers come from source material or stay placeholders
- Organizational specifics: review-submission cycles, inspection hold points, meeting cadence — written plainly, these beat any adjective

## Methods checklist

Common methods in this field; a methods chapter needs this depth or examiners smell invention:

- AHP: how the pairwise matrices were elicited (how many experts, what background), scale used, consistency ratio (CR < 0.1) reported
- Fuzzy comprehensive evaluation: grade set, membership functions, where weights came from
- DEMATEL / ISM: how direct-influence matrices were scored, centrality/cause groups, resulting hierarchy
- System dynamics: boundary assumptions, named feedback loops (at least 2-3 with direction explained), parameter sources, historical fit
- Questionnaire / SEM: instrument origin, distributed vs. valid responses, Cronbach's α, fit indices (CFI, TLI, RMSEA)
- Case study: case selection rationale, data sources (interviews / archives / observation), triangulation
- Simulation: parameter basis, comparison baseline, sensitivity analyses performed

All numbers and parameters come from source material; if absent, placeholders — never invented.
