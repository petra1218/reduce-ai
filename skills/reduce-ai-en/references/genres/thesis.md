# Genre: Thesis / Dissertation

AI-flavored writing in a thesis clusters in fixed places: abstract, introduction, literature review, conclusion. Methods and results chapters are naturally concrete and mostly take care of themselves.

## Abstract

Problem, approach, result (with numbers), one sentence of contribution — then stop. No "This thesis first ... then ... finally ..." roadmap; the table of contents already exists. No "has significant theoretical and practical implications"; the reader judges value from the results.

> Before: This thesis first analyzes ..., then proposes ..., and finally validates through experiments. The results demonstrate that the proposed method has significant theoretical and practical value.
> After: To address [problem], this thesis develops [approach]. Applied to [case]: schedule variance fell from 8.3% to 3.1% (numbers from source material), roughly half that of the benchmark method.

## Introduction

Open on a concrete phenomenon, number, or tension in practice — not on "In recent years, ... has attracted increasing attention", which reviewers recognize on sight. Write significance at a concrete level: what problem, for whom, what happens if it goes unsolved. Delete "Numerous studies have been conducted on this topic" as a transition; go straight into the review with a point of view.

## Literature review

Organize by problem and finding, not by author. "Smith studied ... Jones studied ... Wang studied ..." is a list, not a review. For each cluster of work, three things: what it solved, by what method, what it left open. The open problem should lead into this thesis naturally, so the "research gap" never needs announcing. Citation markers stay as given; paraphrase without changing the claimed finding.

## Conclusion

Summarize only what was done and verified, one claim per piece of evidence the examiner can find in the body. No promotion beyond the results. Limitations are specific ("the sample covers 12 projects", "rainy-season conditions were excluded"), not boilerplate humility. Future work: one or two concrete, feasible steps — or none, stated honestly.

## Language details

- Self-reference: "this thesis" / "this study" / "we" — pick one convention and hold it throughout.
- Tense: methods and experiments in past tense ("We surveyed 62 practitioners"); established facts and your conclusions in present ("Cost overruns cluster in design-change orders"). Consistency beats cleverness.
- One English variant (UK or US) throughout; don't mix "behaviour" and "behavior".
- Cross-references land next to the claim they support ("see Fig. 3.2"), not banked at the end of a paragraph.
- Chapter length matches content. No padding to hit a page count; no compressing three paragraphs into one to look concise.
